# 🇫🇷 30-Day French Plan — B1 Push (English/Hindi → Speaking + Listening)

**Goal:** By Day 30, you can hold an extended spoken conversation with connected reasoning (introduce yourself, narrate past events using imparfait/passé composé correctly, express future plans, give opinions with justification, use conditional/subjunctive in common patterns) and understand near-native podcasts/news at a manageable pace. This targets **strong A2 → solid B1**, not just A1.

**Daily time commitment: ~2.5–3 hours/day**, structured as:
- **50 min** — Learn new grammar + vocabulary (all content is written directly inside each day below, nothing to look up elsewhere)
- **10 min** — B1 Boost section (extra advanced grammar layered onto the day's topic — this is what pushes you past A1/A2)
- **30 min** — Listening practice (podcast/YouTube — suggestions given each day)
- **45 min** — Speaking practice out loud (say every example sentence out loud, not just read it)
- **25 min** — Flashcard review (use Anki — add every new word/verb form from today's session, including B1 Boost content)

> **Golden rule:** Say everything out loud. Reading silently will NOT train your mouth or your ear. Every single example sentence in this document should be spoken aloud at least twice before moving on.

> **Anki tip:** Create one deck called "French". Every day, add the new words/verb-forms listed in that day's "Add to Anki" box. Review due cards for 20 min daily (the app schedules this automatically).

### 📅 Day 1 — Pronunciation, Greetings, and First Sentences

#### Part 1: Pronunciation Rules (learn these first — everything else depends on this)

| Letter/Combo | Sound (approx.) | Example | Say it |
|---|---|---|---|
| e (at word end) | usually silent | parle → "parl" | parle |
| é | "ay" (like "day") | café | café |
| è / ê | "eh" (like "bed") | mère | mère |
| u | tight "ee" with rounded/pursed lips (no English equivalent) | tu, une | tu, une |
| ou | "oo" (like "food") | vous | vous |
| oi | "wah" | moi, toi | moi, toi |
| eu | "uh" (like French "the") | deux | deux |
| ch | "sh" | chat | chat |
| j | "zh" (like "s" in "measure") | je | je |
| r | throaty, from the back of throat (like light gargling) | merci | merci |
| gn | "ny" (like Spanish ñ) | montagne | montagne |
| an/en/am/em | nasal "ah" (push air through nose, don't fully pronounce n) | enfant | enfant |
| in/im/ain/ein | nasal "eh" (through nose) | vin, pain | vin, pain |
| on/om | nasal "oh" (through nose) | bon | bon |
| un/um | nasal "uh" | un | un |
| **Liaison** | last consonant links to next word if it starts with vowel | vous_avez → "voo-za-vay" | vous avez |
| **Silent final letters** | Most final consonants (s, t, d, x, p) are silent | Paris → "Pa-ree" | Paris |

**Speaking drill:** Say each row's example 5 times out loud before moving on. Spend 15 minutes just on this table — pronunciation habits formed now will stick for the whole month.

| Bonsoir | Good evening |

#### Part 3: Your First Sentences (memorize these exactly)
- **Bonjour, je m'appelle [your name].** — Hello, my name is [name].
- **Comment allez-vous?** — How are you? (formal, use with strangers/elders)
- **Ça va bien, merci. Et vous?** — I'm doing well, thanks. And you?
- **Enchanté(e) de faire votre connaissance.** — Pleased to meet you.
- **Au revoir, à bientôt!** — Goodbye, see you soon!

#### Speaking Task (35 min)
1. Say the full pronunciation table out loud 2 full passes (10 min).
2. Practice the 5 memorized sentences above until you can say them without reading (15 min). Record yourself on your phone.
3. Have an imaginary intro exchange: greet, ask how someone is, respond, say nice to meet you, say goodbye — do this as one flowing mini-conversation, both roles, out loud (10 min).

#### Listening Task (25 min)
- Search YouTube for **"Coffee Break French Episode 1"** or **"French for beginners greetings"**. Listen once through without pausing. Then listen again, pausing after each phrase, repeating it out loud (shadowing).

#### B1 Boost: Register & Rhythm
French sentences have even, flowing stress (unlike English's up-down stress) — the LAST syllable of a phrase gets slight emphasis, not random syllables. Practice: "Je m'appelle Anant" — stress falls on "-pelle" and "-nant" lightly, not the first syllable like English would.
Also learn the formal register alternative for "Enchanté(e)": **"Ravi(e) de vous rencontrer."** (Delighted to meet you — more formal/professional.)

### 📅 Day 2 — Subject Pronouns + Être (to be)

#### Part 1: Subject Pronouns
| French | English | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| je | I | becomes "j'" before a vowel (j'aime) |
| tu | you | informal singular — friends, family, kids |
| il / elle / on | he / she / one | "on" is very commonly used to mean "we" in spoken French |
| nous | we | formal "we" |
| vous | you | formal singular, OR plural (any group) |
| ils / elles | they | "ils" for masc./mixed groups, "elles" for all-feminine groups |

**Important cultural note:** Use **tu** with friends, family, children, peers. Use **vous** with strangers, elders, in professional settings. When in doubt, use vous — French speakers appreciate the respect.

#### Part 2: Être (to be) — the most important verb in French
| Pronoun | Form | Example sentence |
|---|---|---|
| je | suis | Je suis étudiant(e). (I am a student.) |
| tu | es | Tu es fatigué(e). (You are tired.) |
| il/elle/on | est | Elle est heureuse. (She is happy.) On est amis. (We are friends.) |
| nous | sommes | Nous sommes en France. (We are in France.) |
| vous | êtes | Vous êtes gentil(le). (You are kind.) |
| ils/elles | sont | Ils sont contents. (They are happy.) |

**Why être matters so much:** It's used for identity, nationality, profession, emotions, and locations, and it's a building block for later grammar (passé composé in Week 3 uses être too).

#### Part 3: Practice Sentences (say each aloud, then write it once)
1. Je suis indien(ne). (I am Indian.)
2. Tu es mon ami(e). (You are my friend.)
3. Il est grand. Elle est petite. (He is tall. She is short.)
4. Nous sommes fatigués. (We are tired.)
5. Vous êtes très gentil. (You are very kind.)
6. Ils sont à la maison. (They are at home.)
7. Je ne suis pas français(e). (I am not French.) — preview of negation, more on Day 5.

#### Speaking Task (35 min)
- Say the full être conjugation table 3 times through, out loud, without looking after the 3rd time (test yourself).
- Create 5 of your own original sentences using être with different pronouns describing yourself, a friend, and your family. Speak each one twice.

#### Listening Task (25 min)
- Continue with Coffee Break French Ep. 1–2, or search "French être verb listening practice." Try to catch every time you hear "je suis," "tu es," "il est," etc.

#### B1 Boost: "C'est" vs "Il/Elle est"
Both translate "he/she/it is" but differ: use **c'est** + article/noun/name/adjective when generalizing ("C'est un professeur." "C'est facile." "C'est Marie."); use **il/elle est** + adjective/profession alone, no article ("Elle est professeure." "Il est grand."). This distinction trips up learners for years — start noticing it now.

### 📅 Day 3 — Avoir (to have) + Numbers 0–20

#### Part 1: Avoir (to have)
| Pronoun | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| j' | ai | J'ai un frère. (I have a brother.) |
| tu | as | Tu as une voiture. (You have a car.) |
| il/elle/on | a | Elle a deux enfants. (She has two children.) |
| nous | avons | Nous avons du temps. (We have time.) |
| vous | avez | Vous avez raison. (You are right — lit. "you have reason") |
| ils/elles | ont | Ils ont faim. (They are hungry — lit. "they have hunger") |

**Key idiom pattern:** French uses **avoir** where English uses "to be" for many feelings: j'ai faim (I'm hungry), j'ai soif (I'm thirsty), j'ai chaud/froid (I'm hot/cold), j'ai peur (I'm scared), j'ai raison/tort (I'm right/wrong), j'ai ans (I am ___ years old — j'ai 25 ans).

#### Part 2: Numbers 0–20
zéro(0), un(1), deux(2), trois(3), quatre(4), cinq(5), six(6), sept(7), huit(8), neuf(9), dix(10), onze(11), douze(12), treize(13), quatorze(14), quinze(15), seize(16), dix-sept(17), dix-huit(18), dix-neuf(19), vingt(20)

#### Part 3: Practice Sentences
1. J'ai vingt ans. (I am 20 years old.)
2. Tu as trois frères? (Do you have three brothers?)
3. Il a faim, elle a soif. (He is hungry, she is thirsty.)
4. Nous avons deux chats. (We have two cats.)
5. Vous avez raison. (You're right.)
6. Ils ont peur. (They are scared.)

#### Speaking Task (35 min)
- Recite numbers 0-20 forward, then backward, 3 times each (10 min).
- Say the avoir table 3 times through (10 min).
- Say your actual age, your phone number digit-by-digit, and how many siblings you have, all in French (15 min).

#### Listening Task (25 min)
- Find a "counting in French 0-20" video and a beginner podcast episode. Focus on catching numbers whenever spoken.

#### B1 Boost: More Avoir Idioms + "Il y a"
Add these high-frequency idioms: **avoir besoin de** (to need), **avoir envie de** (to feel like/want), **avoir l'air (de)** (to seem/look like), **avoir lieu** (to take place), **avoir du mal à** (to have trouble doing). Also learn **il y a** = "there is/are": "Il y a un problème." (There's a problem.) "Il y a trois personnes." (There are 3 people.) Same phrase also means "ago": "Il y a deux ans" (Two years ago).
Practice: J'ai besoin d'aide. Elle a envie de voyager. Il y a beaucoup de monde ici. Il a l'air fatigué.

#### Add to Anki
All 6 avoir forms, numbers 0-20, the idiom list (j'ai faim, j'ai soif, j'ai chaud, j'ai froid, j'ai peur, j'ai raison, j'ai tort), plus the 5 B1 Boost idioms + il y a with both meanings.

### 📅 Day 4 — Aller, Faire + Days of the Week

#### Part 1: Aller (to go)
| Pronoun | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| je | vais | Je vais à l'école. (I go to school.) |
| tu | vas | Tu vas au marché. (You go to the market.) |
| il/elle/on | va | On va au cinéma. (We're going to the cinema.) |
| nous | allons | Nous allons en France. (We are going to France.) |
| vous | allez | Vous allez bien? (Are you doing well? — idiom) |
| ils/elles | vont | Ils vont au travail. (They go to work.) |

**Note:** "Comment ça va?" / "Ça va?" literally uses aller — "how does it go" = "how are you."

#### Part 2: Faire (to do/make)
| Pronoun | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| je | fais | Je fais du sport. (I do sports/exercise.) |
| tu | fais | Tu fais la cuisine. (You cook.) |
| il/elle/on | fait | Il fait ses devoirs. (He does his homework.) |
| nous | faisons | Nous faisons une pause. (We take a break.) |
| vous | faites | Vous faites du yoga. (You do yoga.) |
| ils/elles | font | Elles font du shopping. (They go shopping.) |

**Note:** Faire is also used for weather (il fait beau = it's nice out) — you'll use this in Week 2.

#### Part 3: Days of the Week
lundi (Mon), mardi (Tue), mercredi (Wed), jeudi (Thu), vendredi (Fri), samedi (Sat), dimanche (Sun)
- "Le lundi" = "on Mondays" (habitual). "Lundi" alone = "this/next Monday."

#### Part 4: Practice Sentences
1. Je vais à l'école le lundi. (I go to school on Mondays.)
2. Tu vas au travail. (You go to work.)
3. Nous faisons du sport le samedi. (We exercise on Saturdays.)
4. Elle va au marché le dimanche. (She goes to the market on Sundays.)
5. Ils font leurs devoirs. (They do their homework.)

#### Speaking Task (35 min)
- Say aller and faire conjugation tables 3 times each.
- Say all 7 days of the week aloud 3 times, forward and backward.
- Make one original sentence per day of the week using either aller or faire (7 sentences total), speak each aloud twice.

#### Listening Task (25 min)
- Continue your podcast series. Listen specifically for "je vais," "on va," "je fais" patterns and day names.

#### B1 Boost: "Venir de + infinitive" (recent past)
This means "to have just done something": **Je viens de manger.** (I just ate.) **Elle vient d'arriver.** (She just arrived.) It uses aller's cousin verb "venir" and pairs perfectly with what you just learned. Note: this is grammatically present tense but expresses an event that JUST happened — a very natural, commonly-used structure in real conversation.
Practice: Je viens de finir mon travail. Nous venons de voir un film. Ils viennent de partir.

6 aller forms, 6 faire forms, 7 days of week, 5 practice sentences, plus "venir de + infinitive" pattern with 3 examples.

### 📅 Day 5 — Negation + Numbers 21–100

#### Part 1: Negation Rule
Wrap the conjugated verb with **ne ... pas**:
- Je parle → **Je ne parle pas** (I do not speak)
- Il est → **Il n'est pas** (before a vowel sound, "ne" shortens to "n'")
- Tu as → **Tu n'as pas**

More negation words (useful, learn to recognize them): ne...jamais (never), ne...rien (nothing), ne...plus (no longer/not anymore), ne...personne (nobody)

#### Part 2: Numbers 21–100
vingt et un(21), vingt-deux(22)...vingt-neuf(29), trente(30), trente et un(31)... quarante(40), cinquante(50), soixante(60), **soixante-dix(70)** [lit. "sixty-ten"], soixante et onze(71), soixante-douze(72)... **quatre-vingts(80)** [lit. "four-twenties"], quatre-vingt-un(81)... **quatre-vingt-dix(90)** [lit. "four-twenty-ten"], quatre-vingt-onze(91)... cent(100)

**Note:** The 70/80/90 pattern is famously irregular — practice it slowly. Belgian/Swiss French uses septante(70)/nonante(90) but standard France French uses the pattern above.

#### Part 3: Practice Sentences
1. Je ne suis pas fatigué(e). (I'm not tired.)
2. Tu n'as pas raison. (You're not right.)
3. Il ne va pas au travail aujourd'hui. (He's not going to work today.)
4. Nous n'avons pas d'argent. (We don't have money.) — note "de/d'" replaces "un/une" after negation
5. J'ai soixante-quinze ans. (I am 75 years old.)
6. Ça coûte quatre-vingt-dix euros. (That costs 90 euros.)

#### Speaking Task (35 min)
- Take 8 sentences you made on Days 2-4 and convert each to negative form (15 min).
- Count aloud by 10s from 10 to 100, then count 70-100 slowly and carefully 3 times since it's the hardest part (15 min).
- Speak the 6 practice sentences above (5 min).

#### Listening Task (25 min)
- Watch a video with subtitles this time (search "Easy French numbers" or continue podcast). Pause and repeat any negated sentences or numbers you hear.

#### B1 Boost: More Negation + Ordinal Numbers
Add: **ne...que** (only — "Je n'ai que dix euros" = I only have 10 euros), **ne...ni...ni** (neither...nor — "Je n'aime ni le café ni le thé"). Also learn ordinals: premier/première(1st), deuxième(2nd), troisième(3rd), quatrième(4th), cinquième(5th), dixième(10th) — pattern is number + "-ième" (with -e dropped first if present), except "premier/première."
Practice: C'est mon premier jour. Elle habite au deuxième étage. Je ne bois ni thé ni café.

### 📅 Day 6 — Questions + Family Vocabulary

#### Part 1: Three Ways to Ask Questions
1. **Rising intonation** (informal/spoken): "Tu parles français?" — just raise your pitch at the end, no words change.
2. **Est-ce que + statement** (neutral, most common): "Est-ce que tu parles français?"
3. **Inversion** (formal/written): "Parles-tu français?" — swap verb and pronoun, add hyphen.

#### Part 2: Question Words
| French | English |
|---|---|
| qui | who |
| que / quoi | what |
| où | where |
| quand | when |
| pourquoi | why |
| comment | how |
| combien (de) | how much/many |
| quel/quelle | which/what |

Example combos: "Où est-ce que tu habites?" (Where do you live?), "Pourquoi tu es fatigué?" (Why are you tired?), "Combien de frères as-tu?" (How many brothers do you have?)

#### Part 3: Family Vocabulary
| French | English |
|---|---|
| homme / femme | man / woman |
| garçon / fille | boy / girl |
| père / mère | father / mother |
| frère / sœur | brother / sister |
| fils / fille | son / daughter |
| grand-père / grand-mère | grandfather / grandmother |
| oncle / tante | uncle / aunt |
| cousin / cousine | cousin (m/f) |
| mari / femme | husband / wife |
| ami(e) | friend |
| enfant | child |
| famille | family |
| parents | parents |

#### Part 4: Practice — Write and speak 5 questions about family, then answer them
1. As-tu des frères et sœurs? — Oui, j'ai un frère et une sœur.
2. Comment s'appelle ton père? — Il s'appelle...
3. Où habite ta famille? — Ma famille habite à...
4. Combien de personnes y a-t-il dans ta famille? — Il y a... personnes.
5. Quel âge a ta mère? — Elle a... ans.

#### Speaking Task (35 min)
- Practice all 3 question formats using the same base sentence, out loud (10 min).
- Say the family vocabulary list 3 times through (10 min).
- Ask and answer all 5 questions above out loud, playing both roles (15 min).

#### Listening Task (25 min)
- Watch an "Easy French" street-interview style video (has subtitles) — focus on catching family-related words and question patterns.

#### B1 Boost: Intro to Relative Pronouns (qui / que)
These join two sentences into one — essential for sounding fluent instead of choppy. **qui** = subject of the next clause (who/which): "J'ai un frère **qui** habite à Paris." **que** = object of the next clause (that/whom): "Le livre **que** je lis est intéressant." Rule of thumb: if the next verb has no subject yet, use qui; if the next clause already has its own subject, use que.
Practice: Ma sœur qui est médecin habite loin. Le film que j'ai vu était bon.

### 📅 Day 8 — Regular -ER Verbs (the biggest verb group)

#### Part 1: The -ER Conjugation Pattern
Remove **-er** from the infinitive, add: **-e, -es, -e, -ons, -ez, -ent**

Example with **parler** (to speak):
| Pronoun | Form |
|---|---|
| je | parl**e** |
| tu | parl**es** |
| il/elle/on | parl**e** |
| nous | parl**ons** |
| vous | parl**ez** |
| ils/elles | parl**ent** |

**Pronunciation note:** je/tu/il/ils forms all sound identical when spoken (the endings -e, -es, -ent are silent)! Only nous (-ons) and vous (-ez) sound different. This makes speaking easier than it looks.

#### Part 2: 20 Common -ER Verbs (this is your core vocabulary engine)
parler (speak), manger (eat), regarder (watch/look), écouter (listen), aimer (like/love), habiter (live/reside), travailler (work), étudier (study), acheter (buy), donner (give), arriver (arrive), chercher (look for), penser (think), demander (ask), jouer (play), voyager (travel), rester (stay), rentrer (return home), commencer (begin), aider (help)

#### Part 3: Practice — Conjugate and use 6 verbs
Pick 6 verbs from the list above. Fully conjugate each (all 6 pronoun forms) in writing, then make one sentence per verb:
- Je parle français et anglais.
- Nous mangeons du riz.
- Il regarde la télévision.
- Vous écoutez de la musique.
- Tu travailles beaucoup.
- Elles voyagent en France.

#### Speaking Task (35 min)
- Say the parler conjugation table 3 times.
- Conjugate 6 verbs out loud (say all 6 forms for each verb without writing, testing memory).
- Speak all 6 example sentences twice each.

#### Listening Task (25 min)
- Podcast episode — listen specifically for -er verb endings (-e, -ons, -ez, -ent sounds).

#### B1 Boost: -IR and -RE Verb Groups (critical gap-filler)
Not every verb is -ER! Two more regular groups:

**-IR verbs** (like finir - to finish): remove -ir, add **-is, -is, -it, -issons, -issez, -issent**
finis, finis, finit, finissons, finissez, finissent. Also: choisir (choose), réfléchir (think/reflect), réussir (succeed), grandir (grow up).

**-RE verbs** (like vendre - to sell): remove -re, add **-s, -s, -[nothing], -ons, -ez, -ent**
vends, vends, vend, vendons, vendez, vendent. Also: attendre (wait), perdre (lose), descendre (go down), répondre (answer), entendre (hear).

Practice: Je finis mon travail à cinq heures. Tu choisis quel restaurant? Nous attendons le bus. Elle répond à la question.

### 📅 Day 9 — Adjective Agreement

#### Part 1: The Rule
Adjectives must match the noun's **gender** (masculine/feminine) and **number** (singular/plural):
- Base rule: add **-e** for feminine, **-s** for plural, **-es** for feminine plural (skip if already ending in that letter)
- petit (m.sg) → petit**e** (f.sg) → petit**s** (m.pl) → petit**es** (f.pl)

#### Part 2: Irregular Adjectives (memorize these — very common)
| Masculine | Feminine | English |
|---|---|---|
| beau | belle | beautiful |
| nouveau | nouvelle | new |
| bon | bonne | good |
| vieux | vieille | old |
| heureux | heureuse | happy |
| gentil | gentille | kind |

#### Part 3: Word Order
Most adjectives go **AFTER** the noun: "une voiture rouge" (a red car), "un livre intéressant" (an interesting book).
A small set of common short adjectives go **BEFORE** the noun — remember with acronym **BAGS** (Beauty, Age, Goodness, Size): beau, joli, jeune, vieux, bon, mauvais, grand, petit, nouveau.
- "un **beau** jardin" (a beautiful garden), "une **petite** maison" (a small house)

#### Part 4: Adjective Vocabulary

#### Part 2: CRITICAL RULE — Agreement
With être verbs, the past participle must agree in **gender and number with the subject** (like an adjective!):
- Il **est allé** au marché. (He went — masculine, no change)
- Elle **est allée** au marché. (She went — add -e for feminine)
- Ils **sont allés** au marché. (They went — masc./mixed plural, add -s)
- Elles **sont allées** au marché. (They went — fem. plural, add -es)

| ils/elles | sont allés | sont allées |

#### B1 Boost: Passé Composé vs Imparfait — Choosing the Right One
This distinction is THE hallmark of intermediate French. Rule of thumb:
- Use **passé composé** for a single completed action, a specific moment, or something that interrupts: "Hier, **j'ai vu** un film." "Soudain, il **est tombé**."
- Use **imparfait** for background description, ongoing states, habits/repeated actions, or "was ___-ing" when interrupted: "Il **faisait** beau quand je **suis sorti**." (It was nice out [background/imparfait] when I went out [action/passé composé].)
- Memory trick: imparfait paints the scene (weather, feelings, age, time, ongoing background); passé composé tells what happened.

#### B1 Boost: Relative Pronouns Expanded (où, dont)

(The book **that** I'm talking about — lit. "of which I speak.")

#### B1 Boost: Subjunctive Mood — Introduction (real B1 marker)
The subjunctive is used after certain triggers expressing necessity, emotion, desire, or doubt — usually after "que." Formation (regular verbs): take the **ils/elles** present form, drop **-ent**, add **-e, -es, -e, -ions, -iez, -ent** (nous/vous forms are identical to imparfait).
Example with parler (ils parl**ent** → parl-): que je parl**e**, que tu parl**es**, qu'il parl**e**, que nous parl**ions**, que vous parl**iez**, qu'ils parl**ent**.
Common triggers: **il faut que** (Day 13's "il faut" + que + subjunctive instead of infinitive), **je veux que**, **je suis content(e) que**.
Practice: Il faut que tu **finisses** tes devoirs. Je veux que tu **viennes**. (venir is irregular in subjunctive: vienne, viennes, vienne, venions, veniez, viennent.)

#### B1 Boost: Conditional Mood — Introduction
The conditional ("would do") uses the SAME stem as futur simple (usually infinitive) + imparfait endings: **-ais, -ais, -ait, -ions, -iez, -aient**. Example with parler: je parler**ais**, tu parler**ais**, il parler**ait**... (I would speak). You already know "je voudrais" (Day 10) — that's conditional of vouloir! Also useful: "je pourrais" (I could, from pouvoir), "j'aimerais" (I would like, from aimer).
Practice: Si j'avais le temps, je voyagerais plus. (If I had time, I would travel more — a "si + imparfait, conditional" structure, very common.)

### 📅 Day 22 — Modal Verbs (vouloir, pouvoir, devoir)

#### Part 1: The Three Modal Verbs
| Pronoun | vouloir (want) | pouvoir (can/be able) | devoir (must/have to) |
|---|---|---|---|
| je | veux | peux | dois |
| tu | veux | peux | dois |
| il/elle/on | veut | peut | doit |
| nous | voulons | pouvons | devons |
| vous | voulez | pouvez | devez |
| ils/elles | veulent | peuvent | doivent |

**Usage:** modal + infinitive verb, just like futur proche:
- Je **veux voyager**. (I want to travel.)
- Je **peux parler** français. (I can speak French.)
- Je **dois travailler**. (I have to work.)

**Polite form reminder:** "je voudrais" (I would like, from Day 10) is more polite than "je veux" (I want) — use voudrais when requesting things.

#### Part 2: Practice — 9 sentences (3 per verb)
1. Je veux apprendre le français.
2. Tu veux manger maintenant?
3. Elle veut voyager en France.
4. Je peux nager.
5. Nous pouvons parler français un peu.
6. Ils peuvent venir ce soir.
7. Je dois étudier ce soir.
8. Tu dois te reposer. (You must rest.)
9. Vous devez partir maintenant.

#### Speaking Task (35 min)
- Say all 3 verb tables, 3 times through each.
- Speak all 9 practice sentences twice each.
- Make 3 new sentences combining a modal verb + an infinitive from Week 1-3 vocab you haven't used yet.

#### Listening Task (25 min)

#### B1 Boost: Full Conditional Conjugation of vouloir/pouvoir/devoir
| Pronoun | voudrais (would want) | pourrais (could) | devrais (should) |
|---|---|---|---|
| je | voudrais | pourrais | devrais |
| tu | voudrais | pourrais | devrais |
| il/elle/on | voudrait | pourrait | devrait |
| nous | voudrions | pourrions | devrions |
| vous | voudriez | pourriez | devriez |
| ils/elles | voudraient | pourraient | devraient |

"Tu **devrais** étudier plus." (You should study more.) "Nous **pourrions** aller au cinéma." (We could go to the cinema.) These conditional modals are used constantly for polite suggestions/advice — arguably more useful in daily conversation than the present tense versions.
